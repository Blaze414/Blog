On 21 April 1856, Melbourne stonemasons stopped work and marched for an eight-hour day. The event became a landmark in Australian labour history, but the change was neither instant nor universal.

## The campaign in three steps

1. **Set the date.** A public meeting at Melbourne’s Queen’s Theatre resolved that the building trades would begin working eight-hour days on 21 April.
2. **Walk out.** Stonemasons left the University of Melbourne building site and marched towards Parliament House, gathering workers from other sites along the way.
3. **Keep negotiating.** In the following months, an agreement gave stonemasons eight-hour days without reducing the pay they had received for ten hours.

## The part worth remembering

The first victory covered only a minority of workers, mainly in building trades. Victoria did not pass an Eight Hours Act until 1916, and a nationwide 40-hour, five-day week was not approved until 1948.

The campaign’s enduring idea was a day divided into equal parts: work, recreation and rest. Melbourne’s Eight Hour Monument, at Russell and Victoria streets, still records that “888” principle.
