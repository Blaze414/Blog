Wagga Wagga did not simply “invent” the Chiko Roll. Its more defensible claim is just as interesting: the city hosted the snack’s documented public breakthrough.

## The story in three bites

1. **Developed in Victoria.** Bendigo boilermaker and caterer Frank McEncroe developed a sturdier, one-handed alternative to a chop-suey roll he had seen outside a Richmond football ground.
2. **Launched in Wagga.** The new roll appeared at the 1951 Wagga Wagga Agricultural Show. The Australian Dictionary of Biography says it was acclaimed there; Wagga Wagga City Council describes the event as its first commercial sale.
3. **Claimed by several places.** Wagga, Bendigo and Bathurst later argued over the snack’s home. The council itself calls Wagga’s “birthplace” claim controversial and records the manufacturer’s position that the origins are varied and complex.

## What Wagga can confidently claim

Wagga was the launch stage, not the whole origin story. For the Chiko Roll’s fiftieth anniversary, its manufacturer presented gold-plated rolls to both Wagga and Bendigo—a fittingly shared memorial to a famously disputed snack.
